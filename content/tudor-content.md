# Meet Henry VIII

Henry VIII became King of England in **1509** and ruled until his death in **1547**.

He was the second Tudor king.

Henry is one of England's most famous monarchs. He is particularly remembered for:

- Having **six wives**
- Wanting a **male heir**
- Breaking with the authority of the Pope
- Changes to the Church in England
- The Dissolution of the Monasteries

---

# Henry VIII and His Six Wives

Henry VIII married **six times**.

His marriages were affected by his desire to have a male heir, politics and religion.

## Catherine of Aragon

**Catherine of Aragon was Henry's first wife.**

They were married for more than 20 years.

Catherine gave birth to a daughter called **Mary**, who later became Queen Mary I.

However, Henry and Catherine did not have a surviving son.

Henry became increasingly concerned about not having a male heir and eventually wanted their marriage ended.

He also wanted to marry **Anne Boleyn**.

The Pope would not grant Henry the annulment he wanted.

---

## Anne Boleyn

**Anne Boleyn was Henry's second wife.**

Henry married Anne after his relationship with Catherine of Aragon broke down.

Anne gave birth to a daughter called **Elizabeth** in 1533.

Elizabeth later became **Queen Elizabeth I**.

Anne did not give Henry the surviving male heir he wanted.

In **1536**, Anne was arrested and imprisoned in the Tower of London.

She was accused of serious crimes against the King, including treason.

Anne was found guilty and **executed in 1536**.

**Your mission is to reach Anne before her execution!**

---

## Jane Seymour

**Jane Seymour was Henry's third wife.**

Jane finally gave Henry the male heir he had wanted.

In **1537**, she gave birth to a son called **Edward**.

Edward later became **King Edward VI**.

Jane died shortly after Edward was born.

---

## Anne of Cleves

**Anne of Cleves was Henry's fourth wife.**

Their marriage was partly arranged for political reasons.

The marriage lasted only a short time and was **annulled**.

Anne survived the marriage and remained in England.

---

## Catherine Howard

**Catherine Howard was Henry's fifth wife.**

Catherine was accused of adultery and treason.

She was imprisoned and later **executed**.

She was the second of Henry's six wives to be executed.

---

## Catherine Parr

**Catherine Parr was Henry's sixth and final wife.**

She was well educated and helped care for Henry's children.

Catherine **survived Henry VIII**.

Henry died in 1547.

---

# Remember the Six Wives

Henry's wives, in order, were:

1. **Catherine of Aragon**
2. **Anne Boleyn**
3. **Jane Seymour**
4. **Anne of Cleves**
5. **Catherine Howard**
6. **Catherine Parr**

A famous rhyme can help you remember what happened to them:

**Divorced, Beheaded, Died, Divorced, Beheaded, Survived**

> The rhyme is a useful memory aid. The marriages to Catherine of Aragon and Anne of Cleves were actually annulled.

# Henry VIII and the Male Heir

Having a **male heir** was extremely important to Henry.

An heir is someone who will inherit the throne.

Henry wanted a legitimate son who could become king after his death.

Catherine of Aragon gave Henry a daughter, **Mary**, but no surviving son.

Anne Boleyn gave Henry another daughter, **Elizabeth**.

It was Henry's third wife, **Jane Seymour**, who finally gave birth to his son **Edward** in 1537.

When Henry died in 1547, Edward became **King Edward VI**.

---

# Henry VIII's Children

Three of Henry's children eventually ruled England.

## Mary I

**Mary** was the daughter of Catherine of Aragon.

She later became **Queen Mary I**.

## Elizabeth I

**Elizabeth** was the daughter of Anne Boleyn.

She later became **Queen Elizabeth I**.

## Edward VI

**Edward** was the son of Jane Seymour.

He was the male heir Henry had wanted and became **King Edward VI** after Henry's death.

---

# Henry VIII and the Pope

Henry's marriage problems eventually contributed to major religious changes in England.

Henry wanted his marriage to Catherine of Aragon annulled so that he could marry Anne Boleyn.

At the time, England was part of the Roman Catholic Church.

The **Pope** was the head of the Roman Catholic Church.

The Pope would not grant Henry the annulment he wanted.

Henry eventually rejected the **Pope's authority in England**.

---

# The English Reformation

Henry's break with the authority of the Pope became an important part of the **English Reformation**.

In **1534**, the Act of Supremacy recognised Henry VIII as **Supreme Head of the Church of England**.

This meant that the English king, rather than the Pope, had authority over the Church in England.

This was a major change in the relationship between the monarchy, religion and the Church.

---

# The Dissolution of the Monasteries

Another major change during Henry's reign was the **Dissolution of the Monasteries**.

Monasteries were religious communities where monks or nuns lived and worshipped.

They also controlled land and wealth.

Between **1536 and 1540**, Henry's government closed monasteries across England.

Their land, buildings and wealth were taken by the Crown, with much of the property later sold or granted to others.

The Dissolution increased the land and wealth controlled by Henry and his government and caused major changes across England.
